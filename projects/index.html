Fast simulation of nonlinear deep resistive networks for energy-based computation.

Code

In this work, I extend the coordinate-descent approach introduced by Benjamin Scellier in A fast algorithm to simulate nonlinear resistive networks to deep resistive networks with realistic nonlinearities. This enables the simulation of very large, realistic networks: comparisons against SPICE show speed-ups of up to 1,400× while maintaining high accuracy.

Paper in brief

Deep resistive networks compute by relaxing a nonlinear electrical circuit to equilibrium. Simulating that equilibrium repeatedly is a major bottleneck during training, and existing fast coordinate-descent methods were limited to ideal-diode models.

I generalize the method to realistic monotone device laws: single Shockley diodes, antiparallel double-Shockley diodes, and tabulated piecewise-linear current–voltage curves. Each node update remains a local Kirchhoff-current-law solve, so alternate network layers can be updated efficiently in parallel.

On one- to three-hidden-layer Digits networks, the solver stays below 1.1 × 10−4 P90 relative voltage error against SPICE. It also makes 100-epoch training of a 1568 × 100 × 20 nonlinear network on MNIST practical, reaching about 3% test error.
