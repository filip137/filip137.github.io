Fast simulation of nonlinear deep resistive networks for energy-based computation.

Code

I applied the coordinate-descent approach introduced by Benjamin Scellier to networks with realistic nonlinearities.

Paper in brief

Deep resistive networks are electronic implementations of energy-based computation whose layered topology mirrors deep neural networks. Conductances encode trainable parameters, nonlinear devices act as activation functions, and node voltages settle according to Kirchhoff’s current law. For monotone nonlinearities, this equilibrium minimizes an electrical content function, making the circuits compatible with Equilibrium Propagation and local learning from free and nudged states.

Before these systems can be built at useful scale, they must be trained and tested in simulation—often requiring millions of equilibrium solves. SPICE provides reliable reference solutions, but treats each solve as a generic nonlinear circuit problem and does not exploit the layered structure or repeated nature of DRN computation. This makes large nonlinear DRNs prohibitively slow to study.
